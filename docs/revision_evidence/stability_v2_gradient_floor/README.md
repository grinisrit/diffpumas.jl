# Fixed-objective stationarity and approximate-adjoint failure

These immutable runs diagnose a deterministic derivative limitation. They are not evidence of KKT convergence, uniqueness, stochastic transport accuracy, or calibrated uncertainty. The material correction was disabled; every fit used the same frozen dry correction, 32 midpoint energy nodes, 600 cells, 147 supported cells, 1003 covered bins and 690 retained fit bins. The objective was fixed pseudo-Huber log-flux loss with profiled normalization and a quadratic graph penalty.

`completed_start015` finished with clean source/runtime audits after 1290.9 seconds. Its 18 outer iterations reduced the objective from 160.1108563 to 98.53429563, but the projected-gradient norm was 0.001787599, above the prespecified 1e-6 threshold. Completion did not establish stationarity.

`stopped_fast_start0` used the subsequent exact cheap residual callback, live diagnostics and safeguarded projected descent. The coordinator stopped it after 26 iterations because the objective reached 98.53429565 while the projected-gradient norm continued to oscillate near 0.002–0.004. The final live value was 0.001664251. The wrapper records return code -15 and unchanged source/runtime; this partial run has no final reconstructed-field artifact or stationarity result. Its explicit cancellation reason and live trace are retained.

`stationarity_audit` evaluates the completed 0.15-start field. At cell 275, w=0.1147192654, the reported objective gradient is +0.001787599. Whole-objective central differences at 1e-7 and 1e-8 instead give approximately -0.0036265. The objective value and projected-gradient norm reproduce the saved fit exactly. The inferred absolute graph-penalty weight is 3.95493020127.

`flux_gradient_audit` isolates the discrepancy to one of nine affected rays: bin 916, zenith 70 degrees, azimuth 357.9 degrees and slant distance 3391.45 m. Sparse and full-vector predictions agree exactly; sparse and full-vector reverse derivatives also agree exactly. The default segment-adjoint difference step of 1e-6 gives flux derivative 3.180160130e-6, while whole-flux differences at 1e-8 give 3.156905984e-6, about 0.737% lower. This ray contributes about +0.00541419 to the objective-gradient error. The prior gradient is -0.03836322295; the default data gradient is +0.04015082206.

Changing only the process-local internal adjoint step to 1e-7 or 1e-8 gives data gradient +0.03473666 and total gradient approximately -0.00362656, agreeing with the small-step whole-flux differences. These results are consistent with the existing finite-difference stencil spanning a local adaptive/interpolation branch; its numerical derivative floor can dominate the residual objective gradient near a solution. Merely adding optimization passes or lowering the stopping tolerance cannot certify stationarity under this mismatch. A more accurate derivative implementation requires separate validation and fresh reconstruction runs.
