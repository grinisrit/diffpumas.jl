# Same-reference historical material correction comparison

The final coefficients fitted to independent 8,192-sample training contrasts are applied to the immutable, previously inspected corrected-validation MC references. No stochastic reference is recomputed or refitted. All 576 derivative rows and 168 flux rows pass raw output/source/runtime hash checks. `derived_provenance.json` binds the separately derived summary.

On the same 21 wet rays, median absolute relative flux bias decreases from 27.757% to 12.587%, and maximum from 37.366% to 20.484%. Eighteen of 21 residual means still exceed twice their conditional replicate standard error. On the same 72 derivative comparisons, median absolute relative discrepancy (normalized by absolute replicate-mean MC secant) decreases from 41.877% to 34.575%, and the count exceeding twice the paired-energy discrepancy standard error decreases from 20 to 12. Near-zero MC derivatives produce unstable relative ratios. These observations show improvement and material remaining error; they do not establish physical gradient accuracy or water-recovery accuracy.

The source altitude remains fixed at 30 km for controlled attribution. See the adjacent baseline_control evidence: disabling the new material correction reproduces all 42 old dry/wet CSDA predictions exactly. Previously inspected historical fields are not independent blind validation; the new preregistered direction/field/stream split is evaluated separately. Conditional standard errors omit calibration-coefficient uncertainty and cross-bin covariance and have no calibrated confidence-coverage claim.
